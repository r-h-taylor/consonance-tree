About

Each positive rational a/b is a node of the full Stern–Brocot tree, rooted at 1/1. A musical sequence is a trajectory through the tree. The operators come in two families. Anchor-shifting operators move the trajectory as a coherent shape by shifting its anchor — the deepest common ancestor of its nodes — while preserving each node's position relative to that anchor: L moves the anchor to its left child, R to its right child, U to its parent. Pointwise operators act on each node's address independently: σ swaps L↔R in every address (reflecting the trajectory across 1/1), and rev reverses each address. Together L, R, U can shift any trajectory to any position; every operator preserves the trajectory's internal shape and depth profile. The cents column shows deviation of each just ratio from its nearest 12-TET semitone (±0–10 clean, ±10–25 noticeable, ±25+ 12-TET cannot represent it). The note column includes octave markers ↑n / ↓n when a pitch is n octaves above or below the root.

The tree is drawn with 1/1 at the top centre and the two halves of the Stern–Brocot tree spreading symmetrically: the lower half (intervals < 1) on the left, the upper half (intervals > 1) on the right. The major triad 1/1, 5/4, 3/2 shows root, major third, perfect fifth.

The harmonic frame above each trajectory is the unique pair of Farey neighbors p/q ◁ ▷ r/s (i.e., satisfying qr − ps = 1) that bracket the chord's pitch range as tightly as possible. It is the chord's natural enclosing region in harmonic space — invariant under the chord's internal voicing, and computable directly from the input fractions by a single Stern–Brocot descent. A chord anchored at 3/2 has frame 1/1 ◁ ▷ 2/1 (the octave); one anchored at 5/3 has frame 3/2 ◁ ▷ 2/1 (the fifth-to-octave window). Rooted chords (those containing 1/1) have frame 0 ◁ ▷ ∞ — they span all of frequency space and have no tightening bracket. The frame is also the limit of repeated L / R iteration, but is computed analytically, not by iteration.
